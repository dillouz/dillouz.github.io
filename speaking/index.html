Formats.

Every talk ends with time for questions, and the hard ones are welcome.

  • Keynote

    45 to 60 minutes, with Q&A. Galas, conferences, federation and community events.

  • Community talk or weekend

    One talk, or three to four sessions. Synagogues, JCCs and community weekends.

  • Shabbat scholar weekend

    Friday night to Saturday night. Friday night talk, Shabbat morning drasha, a Q&A at seudah shlishit, and an optional motzei Shabbat talk.

  • Churches

    A short greeting to a full evening. A greeting from Jerusalem at a Sunday service, a 20 to 30 minute address at an evening for Israel, or a Q&A with church leaders.

  • Campus conversation

    Talk and Q&A. Open, registration-only or students-only events, with questions by open mic, written cards or a moderator, as your campus requires.

  • Moderated conversation or panel

    Any length. On stage with a host, or with other speakers.

  • Briefing on Zoom

    30 to 60 minutes. Boards, donors, members and national calls.

  • Briefing in Israel

    45 to 90 minutes. Missions and delegations visiting Israel.

Questions organizers ask.

What does a talk cost?

Fees depend on the format and travel. I work with community, campus and conference budgets, so tell me yours in the form.

What about security?

I've spoken on campuses where Israel is a hard subject. I follow your venue's security plan and can work with registration-only events, written questions or a moderator.

Is the talk partisan?

No. I explain; I don't campaign. If you ask where I stand, I'll tell you, and questions from every side are welcome.

Where are you based, and do you travel?

I live in Jerusalem and travel for talks anywhere. Booking alongside other dates on a tour shares the travel costs.

Can you come for Shabbat?

Yes. I arrive before Shabbat, stay within walking distance of the shul, and speak without a microphone. There's no recording until after Havdalah.

Do you speak in churches?

Yes, gladly: at a Sunday service, an evening for Israel or a meeting with church leaders.

Which languages?

English, Hebrew and French.

What do you need on the day?

A lapel microphone, a handheld one for questions, and HDMI if there are slides. For Zoom, a test call 15 minutes before.

Can we record the talk?

Usually yes, for your members. Please ask when you book.
